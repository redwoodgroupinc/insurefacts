Medicare is the federal health insurance program in the United States. It is divided into four parts, labeled A to D, and each part covers different kinds of care. Knowing what each part does makes it easier to follow the rest of Medicare’s rules.

## Who Medicare is for

Medicare is health insurance for:

- people aged 65 or older
- certain people under 65 with disabilities
- people of any age with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD), which is permanent kidney failure that requires dialysis or a kidney transplant [[2]](#source-2)

People under 65 who get Social Security disability benefits generally get Part A and Part B automatically after receiving those benefits for 24 months. [[8]](#source-8)

## Part A: hospital insurance

Part A helps cover:

- inpatient care in hospitals
- skilled nursing facility care
- hospice care
- some home health care [[1]](#source-1)

Part A does not cover custodial or long-term care. [[3]](#source-3) Most people don’t pay a monthly premium for Part A. [[6]](#source-6)

## Part B: medical insurance

Part B helps cover:

- services from doctors and other health care providers
- outpatient care
- home health care
- durable medical equipment, such as wheelchairs, walkers and hospital beds
- many preventive services [[1]](#source-1)

Part B has a monthly premium and a yearly deductible. The standard amounts are set each year and published on Medicare.gov. [[6]](#source-6)

Together, Part A and Part B are known as **Original Medicare**. [[4]](#source-4)

## Part C: Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage, also called Part C, is a Medicare-approved plan offered by a private company. It is an alternative to Original Medicare for getting Part A and Part B coverage, and most plans also include Part D drug coverage. [[1]](#source-1) [[5]](#source-5)

Medicare Advantage plans work differently from Original Medicare. In many plans, you generally need to use doctors in the plan’s network, and you may need approval from the plan (prior authorization) before getting certain drugs or services. Most plans also offer some extra benefits that Original Medicare doesn’t cover, such as vision, hearing and dental services. [[5]](#source-5)

## Part D: prescription drug coverage

Part D helps cover the cost of prescription drugs, including many brand-name and generic drugs. It is provided by private, Medicare-approved plans. You can get Part D by joining a separate drug plan alongside Original Medicare, or as part of a Medicare Advantage plan that includes drug coverage. [[1]](#source-1) [[5]](#source-5)

## Medigap: Medicare Supplement Insurance

Medigap, or Medicare Supplement Insurance, is not one of the four parts of Medicare. It is extra insurance sold by private companies that helps pay some of the costs Original Medicare doesn’t, such as copayments, coinsurance and deductibles. Medigap works with Original Medicare, not with Medicare Advantage. [[5]](#source-5) [[7]](#source-7)

There is a one-time, six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period that starts when a person is 65 or older and enrolled in Part B. During this period, insurers can’t refuse to sell a Medigap policy or charge more because of health problems. [[7]](#source-7)

## Where to check the details

Medicare costs, coverage rules and plan options change from year to year. Medicare.gov publishes the current premiums and deductibles [[6]](#source-6), and the official Medigap guide explains the rules for Medicare Supplement Insurance in more detail. [[7]](#source-7)
