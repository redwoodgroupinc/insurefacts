Car insurance is compulsory in the UK. The law sets a minimum level of cover for anyone who drives, and it also requires vehicles to stay insured while they are registered for use on the road. This article explains the legal minimum, the usual levels of cover, and what happens if a vehicle isn’t insured.

## The legal minimum: third party insurance

You must have motor insurance to drive a vehicle on UK roads, and third party insurance is the legal minimum. [[1]](#source-1) The requirement comes from Part VI of the Road Traffic Act 1988. [[7]](#source-7)

Third party insurance covers your legal liability to other people, such as injury to them or damage to their vehicle or property. It doesn’t cover other costs, such as repairs to your own vehicle. [[1]](#source-1)

## The three usual levels of cover

Car insurance in the UK is usually sold at three levels:

- **Third party only:** the legal minimum, covering injury to others and damage to their property.
- **Third party, fire and theft:** third party cover, plus cover if your own car is damaged by fire or stolen.
- **Comprehensive:** the highest level, which also covers damage to your own car, depending on the policy terms. [[5]](#source-5)

MoneyHelper notes that third party only cover isn’t necessarily the cheapest option: depending on things such as claims history and where you live, it can cost more than a policy with more cover. [[5]](#source-5) What each policy includes, its excess and any extras depend on the insurer and the policy wording.

## Penalties for driving without insurance

According to GOV.UK, the police can give you a fixed penalty of £300 and 6 penalty points if you’re caught driving a vehicle you’re not insured to drive. If the case goes to court, you could get an unlimited fine and be disqualified from driving. [[2]](#source-2)

The police also have the power to seize, and in some cases destroy, a vehicle that is being driven without insurance. [[2]](#source-2)

Driving without insurance is recorded on a driving licence with the endorsement code IN10. [[4]](#source-4)

## A vehicle must stay insured unless it has a SORN

The rules don’t only apply while a car is being driven. Under a system called continuous insurance enforcement, the registered keeper must keep the vehicle insured, even if it isn’t being used. You don’t need to insure a vehicle if it is kept off the road and declared off the road with a Statutory Off Road Notification (SORN). [[3]](#source-3)

If a vehicle is registered but uninsured and has no SORN, GOV.UK says the keeper could be fined £100, have the vehicle wheel-clamped, impounded or destroyed, or face a court prosecution with a fine of up to £1,000. [[3]](#source-3)

## Checking whether a vehicle is insured

Insurers record insured vehicles on the Motor Insurance Database. You can check whether a vehicle is showing as insured using the askMID service. The askMID result only shows whether the vehicle currently appears on the database; it isn’t proof of insurance on its own. [[6]](#source-6)

## Where to check the details

Penalties and enforcement rules can change. GOV.UK’s vehicle insurance guide sets out the current rules [[1]](#source-1), and your policy documents explain exactly what your own cover includes.
